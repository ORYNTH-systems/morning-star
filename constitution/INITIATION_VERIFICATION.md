Morning Star Initiation Verification Specification

Document Status: Constitutional Candidate
Version: 0.1.0
Specification Identifier: MS-IVS-001

1. Purpose

This specification defines how Morning Star verifies competent understanding before participation or stewardship authority is admitted.

Initiation verification measures structural preservation, not allegiance.

2. Verification Subject

The verification subject is the observer's demonstrated ability to preserve and reconstruct the governed architecture.

The verification subject is not:

personality;
status;
loyalty;
charisma;
vocabulary density;
confidence;
agreement;
imitation of the author;
spiritual identity.
3. Verification Dimensions
IV-01 — Canonical Meaning Preservation

Can the observer state the governed meaning without replacing it with a personal interpretation?

IV-02 — Framework Identity Preservation

Can the observer correctly identify which framework, layer, protocol, runtime, or artifact is being discussed?

IV-03 — Dependency Preservation

Can the observer identify the required antecedent structures and preserve their order?

IV-04 — Boundary Preservation

Can the observer distinguish what the governed object includes from what it explicitly excludes?

IV-05 — Terminological Integrity

Can the observer use canonical terms without collapsing distinct meanings?

IV-06 — Traceability

Can the observer identify the authoritative source and reconstruct the evidentiary path?

IV-07 — Uncertainty Preservation

Can the observer preserve unresolved, provisional, missing, or disputed states without falsely resolving them?

IV-08 — Independent Reconstruction

Can the observer reconstruct the architecture without merely copying a provided summary?

IV-09 — Interpretive Declaration

Can the observer clearly distinguish personal extension, analogy, criticism, or interpretation from canonical ORYNTH content?

IV-10 — Revision Discipline

Can the observer identify when a proposed change requires constitutional authority rather than informal adoption?

4. Verification Method

Verification may use:

structured reconstruction;
dependency mapping;
framework-classification exercises;
boundary analysis;
contradiction analysis;
provenance reconstruction;
semantic-drift detection;
controlled application to a new scenario;
revision-impact assessment.

No single method is sufficient for every governed object.

5. Verification Result

Each dimension receives one of the following:

VERIFIED;
PARTIALLY_VERIFIED;
NOT_VERIFIED;
INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE;
NOT_APPLICABLE;
INDETERMINATE.
6. Admission Rule

For required dimensions D_r:

VerifiedComprehension
=
∀ d ∈ D_r : Result(d) = VERIFIED

A constitutional profile may permit PARTIALLY_VERIFIED results for noncritical dimensions only when the exception is explicit and recorded.

No missing result may be silently interpreted as VERIFIED.

7. Critical Failure Conditions

Verification must fail or remain indeterminate when the observer:

repeatedly merges distinct frameworks;
removes dependency requirements;
substitutes personal interpretation for canonical definition;
erases uncertainty;
fabricates provenance;
attributes unauthorized claims to ORYNTH;
treats narrative correspondence as constitutional proof;
claims authority beyond the assessed domain.
8. Verification Evidence Record

Every verification record must include:

verification identifier;
observer identifier;
governed object;
authoritative sources;
assessed dimensions;
evidence reviewed;
dimension results;
evaluator;
evaluator authority;
timestamp;
unresolved uncertainty;
final disposition;
revalidation requirements.
9. Revalidation

Revalidation is required when:

the governed object materially changes;
constitutional dependencies change;
the observer changes roles;
stewardship authority is requested;
prior evidence becomes unreliable;
semantic drift is detected;
a suspension condition is triggered.
10. Verification Boundary

Verification establishes competence only within the declared governed domain.

It does not establish universal expertise or unrestricted ORYNTH authority.

11. Constitutional Result

Initiation verification establishes whether an observer can participate without materially degrading the semantic integrity of the governed architecture.
