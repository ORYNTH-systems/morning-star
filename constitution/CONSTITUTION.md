## 1. Constitutional Purpose

This Constitution establishes the supreme internal authority governing Morning Star.

Morning Star governs the preservation of canonical meaning from first encounter through competent participation within the ORYNTH ecosystem.

Its constitutional purpose is to prevent semantic drift, identity confusion, dependency inversion, false equivalence, unauthorized reinterpretation, provenance loss, uncertainty suppression, and participation beyond demonstrated admissibility.

No derived Morning Star artifact may contradict, silently expand, or replace this Constitution.

---

## 2. Constitutional Subject

The constitutional subject of Morning Star is:

> The governance of semantic integrity, framework identity, dependency order, interpretive admissibility, role transition, and traceable participation from first contact through competent and authority-bounded participation.

Morning Star governs the conditions under which an observer may:

1. encounter an ORYNTH framework;
2. identify the encountered framework correctly;
3. distinguish it from related but non-equivalent frameworks;
4. navigate its dependencies;
5. interpret its canonical claims;
6. preserve uncertainty and provenance;
7. demonstrate competency;
8. enter an admissible participation role;
9. receive bounded authority;
10. maintain semantic continuity through revision.

## 5. Constitutional Boundary

### 5.1 Entry Boundary

The Morning Star governed process begins when an observer encounters, requests, receives, or is directed toward an ORYNTH framework or governed object.

### 5.2 Transformation Boundary

Within Morning Star, entry information may be transformed through:

- canonical identification;
- prerequisite resolution;
- dependency sequencing;
- interpretation classification;
- uncertainty registration;
- competency evaluation;
- role eligibility evaluation;
- semantic reconstruction;
- revision reconciliation.

### 5.3 Exit Boundary

The governed process reaches a valid exit when the participant possesses:

- correctly identified canonical objects;
- resolved or explicitly unresolved dependencies;
- classified interpretations;
- preserved provenance;
- explicit uncertainty;
- demonstrated role-appropriate competency;
- bounded participation authority;
- a reconstructable participation trace.

### 5.4 Noninterference Rule

Morning Star may determine whether participation is semantically and constitutionally admissible.

Morning Star may not fabricate authority, eliminate unresolved uncertainty, declare substantive framework truth without evidence, or replace the constitutional authority of another framework.

## 10. Constitutional Invariants

The following invariants govern every Morning Star artifact and implementation:

### MS-INV-001 — Canonical Identity Preservation

A canonical object must remain identifiable across presentation, interpretation, implementation, revision, and transfer.

### MS-INV-002 — No Derived Authority Expansion

No interpretation, role, implementation, or derivative artifact may possess authority exceeding its constitutional source.

### MS-INV-003 — Dependency-Order Preservation

A dependent concept may not be treated as independently established when its governing prerequisites remain unresolved.

### MS-INV-004 — Uncertainty Preservation

Unknown, disputed, incomplete, approximate, speculative, and indeterminate states must remain distinguishable.

### MS-INV-005 — Provenance Continuity

Every governed claim, interpretation, transition, correction, and reconstruction must remain traceable to its source and authority.

### MS-INV-006 — Role-Bounded Participation

Participation authority must not exceed demonstrated competency and formally admitted role scope.

### MS-INV-007 — Scope Nonexpansion

No result may be generalized beyond the scope established by its evidence and governing authority.

### MS-INV-008 — Drift Visibility

Detected semantic drift must be recorded and must not be silently normalized into the canonical state.

### MS-INV-009 — Reconstruction Traceability

Semantic reconstruction must preserve the drifted state, corrective basis, authority, and resulting state.

### MS-INV-010 — Revision Propagation

A constitutional revision must identify affected objects, interpretations, roles, dependencies, and derived artifacts.

### MS-INV-011 — Prohibited Conflation

Distinct frameworks, states, roles, authorities, and evidentiary statuses must remain distinguishable unless equivalence is formally established.

### MS-INV-012 — Refusal Before Fabrication

When required meaning, authority, evidence, or dependency cannot be established, Morning Star must preserve unresolved or inadmissible status rather than fabricate completion.

## 14. Drift and Reconstruction

Semantic drift exists when a governed representation no longer preserves one or more of:

- canonical identity;
- constitutional definition;
- dependency order;
- scope;
- authority;
- provenance;
- uncertainty;
- interpretation class;
- role boundary;
- revision state.

Reconstruction must:

1. preserve the drift event;
2. identify the affected object;
3. locate the last admissible state;
4. identify authoritative corrective evidence;
5. restore or replace the affected interpretation;
6. preserve unresolved differences;
7. record the resulting semantic state;
8. trigger required downstream revalidation.

## 15. Framework Admission

A framework may enter Morning Star governance only when it has:

- a stable canonical identity;
- an identified authority;
- a defined constitutional or functional subject;
- declared boundaries;
- known dependencies;
- a version or revision model;
- admissible source material;
- a relationship map to existing frameworks;
- a governing admission decision.

Admission does not establish correctness, superiority, adoption, or universal authority.

Admission establishes that the framework can be represented and navigated without violating Morning Star invariants.
