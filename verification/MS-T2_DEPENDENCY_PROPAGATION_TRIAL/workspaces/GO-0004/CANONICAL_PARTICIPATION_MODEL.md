# Morning Star — Canonical Participation Model

**Document Identifier:** MS-V1-CPM-001  
**Document Status:** Research Candidate  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Parent Volume:** Volume I — Foundation

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the constitutional conditions under which an observer may participate in the ORYNTH ecosystem.

Participation is not established by access, familiarity, agreement, contribution, employment, affiliation, technical skill, or self-declaration.

Participation is a bounded authority state granted only after required competence, semantic convergence, reconstruction capability, traceability, and authority conditions are satisfied.

## 2. Canonical Definition

Canonical participation is the authorized exercise of a defined role within a governed domain by an observer whose competence, state, scope, evidence, and authority have been explicitly established.

Participation is represented as:

\[
P = (O,d,r,s,c,a,e,t,v)
\]

where:

- \(O\) is the participating observer;
- \(d\) is the governed domain;
- \(r\) is the assigned role;
- \(s\) is verified observer state;
- \(c\) is demonstrated competence;
- \(a\) is assigned authority;
- \(e\) is supporting evidence;
- \(t\) is the effective period;
- \(v\) is the governing version.

## 3. Participation Preconditions

An observer is eligible for participation only when:

1. observer identity is established;
2. the governed domain is declared;
3. the applicable framework version is known;
4. the required observer state is verified;
5. semantic convergence is established;
6. reconstruction competence is demonstrated;
7. framework identity is preserved;
8. dependency order is preserved;
9. uncertainty is declared;
10. evidence is traceable;
11. authority is explicitly assigned;
12. no blocking finding remains unresolved.

## 4. Participation Admissibility

For observer \(O\) and domain \(d\), define:

\[
P_A(O,d) =
I_O
\land
S_O
\land
C_O
\land
R_O
\land
F_O
\land
D_O
\land
T_O
\land
U_O
\land
A_O
\]

where:

- \(I_O\) is established identity;
- \(S_O\) is verified observer state;
- \(C_O\) is semantic convergence;
- \(R_O\) is reconstruction competence;
- \(F_O\) is framework differentiation;
- \(D_O\) is dependency preservation;
- \(T_O\) is traceability;
- \(U_O\) is uncertainty preservation;
- \(A_O\) is valid authority.

Participation is admissible only when:

\[
P_A(O,d)=1
\]

## 5. Participation Is Not Authority Creation

Competence does not create authority.

Understanding does not create authority.

Contribution does not create authority.

Therefore:

\[
Competence(O,d) \nRightarrow Authority(O,d)
\]

and:

\[
ParticipationEligibility(O,d)
\nRightarrow
ParticipationAuthority(O,d)
\]

Authority must be constitutionally assigned.

## 6. Participation Classes

| Participation Class | Definition |
|---|---|
| OBSERVATIONAL | May inspect governed materials without altering them. |
| INTERPRETIVE | May produce bounded interpretations with declared scope. |
| RECONSTRUCTIVE | May perform governed semantic reconstruction. |
| CONTRIBUTORY | May propose additions or revisions. |
| IMPLEMENTATION | May implement governed specifications. |
| EVALUATIVE | May perform formal assessment or verification. |
| CORRECTIVE | May execute approved corrections. |
| GOVERNANCE | May participate in governed decisions. |
| STEWARDSHIP | May preserve and administer a governed domain. |
| SUCCESSION | May receive governed transfer of stewardship authority. |

Each class requires an explicit role assignment.

## 7. Participation Scope

Every participation assignment shall define:

- governed domain;
- permitted actions;
- prohibited actions;
- applicable artifacts;
- governing version;
- authority source;
- effective date;
- expiration date;
- revalidation requirements;
- evidence obligations;
- escalation path;
- revocation conditions.

Undefined scope shall not be interpreted broadly.

## 8. Role Assignment

A participation role shall contain:

\[
R_P =
(Id_r,N_r,D_r,S_r,A_r,P_r,E_r,T_r)
\]

where:

- \(Id_r\) is role identifier;
- \(N_r\) is role name;
- \(D_r\) is governed domain;
- \(S_r\) is required observer state;
- \(A_r\) is assigned authority;
- \(P_r\) is prohibited authority;
- \(E_r\) is evidence requirement;
- \(T_r\) is effective period.

Role assignment shall be recorded in `ROLE_ASSIGNMENT_REGISTER.csv`.

## 9. Interpretive Participation

Interpretive participation permits an observer to produce representations of governed meaning.

It requires:

- semantic convergence;
- canonical terminology preservation;
- framework differentiation;
- declared interpretation class;
- declared uncertainty;
- source traceability;
- explicit separation between canonical and noncanonical interpretation.

Interpretive participation does not permit constitutional revision.

## 10. Reconstructive Participation

Reconstructive participation permits an observer to restore canonical meaning from authoritative evidence.

It requires:

- verified reconstruction competence;
- access to authoritative sources;
- preservation of missing and disputed content;
- compliance with semantic drift procedure;
- independent verification where required;
- registration of reconstruction outcomes.

Reconstruction does not permit invention of missing meaning.

## 11. Contributory Participation

Contributory participation permits proposals but not unilateral constitutional alteration.

A contribution shall identify:

- proposed object;
- proposed change;
- rationale;
- evidence;
- affected dependencies;
- compatibility impact;
- uncertainty;
- required review authority.

A contribution remains noncanonical until formally admitted.

## 12. Implementation Participation

Implementation participation permits translation of specification into operational form.

An implementation participant shall preserve:

- governing specification;
- constitutional boundary;
- dependency relationships;
- failure semantics;
- uncertainty semantics;
- traceability;
- version identity;
- verification requirements.

Implementation behavior does not become constitutional authority.

## 13. Evaluative Participation

Evaluative participation permits formal assessment.

The evaluator shall declare:

- evaluation scope;
- authority;
- criteria;
- evidence base;
- conflicts of interest;
- uncertainty;
- result;
- limitations.

Evaluation authority does not imply correction or governance authority.

## 14. Corrective Participation

Corrective participation permits execution of an approved correction.

Correction authority shall specify:

- affected object;
- approved correction;
- evidence basis;
- permitted implementation scope;
- verification requirement;
- closure authority.

Corrective participation shall not expand the approved correction.

## 15. Governance Participation

Governance participation permits bounded involvement in constitutional decisions.

Governance participation requires:

- verified ecosystem competence;
- cross-framework differentiation;
- dependency understanding;
- revision discipline;
- conflict declaration;
- traceable reasoning;
- assigned governance authority.

Governance participation does not automatically establish stewardship.

## 16. Participation Boundaries

Participation boundaries shall distinguish:

- reading from interpretation;
- interpretation from reconstruction;
- reconstruction from revision;
- proposal from approval;
- implementation from specification;
- evaluation from correction;
- participation from governance;
- governance from stewardship;
- stewardship from succession.

No boundary shall be crossed by implication.

## 17. Participation Prohibitions

Morning Star prohibits:

- self-assigned roles;
- implied authority;
- undeclared domain expansion;
- silent version substitution;
- framework conflation;
- authority inferred from technical access;
- authority inferred from authorship alone;
- unrestricted participation based on a single verified domain;
- participation while blocking findings remain unresolved;
- unauthorized alteration of canonical artifacts;
- concealment of uncertainty;
- retrospective fabrication of authority.

## 18. Multi-Role Participation

An observer may hold multiple roles only when each role is independently assigned.

For roles \(r_1\) and \(r_2\):

\[
Assigned(O,r_1)
\nRightarrow
Assigned(O,r_2)
\]

Authority from multiple roles shall not be combined to create powers absent from every individual assignment.

## 19. Collective Participation

A collective participant shall define:

- membership;
- decision process;
- delegated roles;
- evidentiary responsibilities;
- disagreement handling;
- final authority;
- accountability;
- continuity rules.

A collective does not become competent merely because one member is competent.

## 20. Computational Participation

A computational participant may operate only within explicit constraints governing:

- input authority;
- output scope;
- source traceability;
- model or runtime identity;
- version control;
- uncertainty reporting;
- human review;
- prohibited actions;
- execution boundaries;
- auditability.

A computational system may assist interpretation or reconstruction but shall not acquire constitutional authority through performance alone.

## 21. Participation Revalidation

Revalidation is required following:

- material constitutional revision;
- role expiration;
- domain expansion;
- authority change;
- semantic drift finding;
- reconstruction failure;
- dependency change;
- evidence invalidation;
- prolonged inactivity;
- stewardship transition.

## 22. Suspension

Participation may be suspended when:

- evidence becomes insufficient;
- authority is disputed;
- scope is exceeded;
- semantic drift is detected;
- required revalidation is incomplete;
- traceability fails;
- uncertainty is concealed;
- dependency integrity is impaired.

Suspension preserves historical participation records.

## 23. Revocation

Participation authority may be revoked when:

- authority expires;
- assignment is withdrawn;
- constitutional conditions fail;
- role boundaries are materially violated;
- evidence is falsified;
- repeated nonconvergence occurs;
- governance integrity is compromised.

Revocation does not erase prior valid participation.

## 24. Participation Records

Every participation act that materially affects a governed object shall be traceable to:

- observer identity;
- role assignment;
- governed domain;
- authority source;
- applicable version;
- evidence;
- action;
- result;
- timestamp;
- verification status.

## 25. Participation Failure Modes

Participation failure includes:

- false initiation;
- unauthorized interpretation;
- scope expansion;
- role conflation;
- framework conflation;
- dependency inversion;
- authority inflation;
- uncertainty erasure;
- untraceable action;
- stale verification;
- invalid delegation;
- implementation substitution;
- unapproved revision.

## 26. Participation and Stewardship

Participation is necessary but not sufficient for stewardship.

\[
Participation(O,d)
\nRightarrow
Stewardship(O,d)
\]

Stewardship additionally requires:

- governance competence;
- correction competence;
- continuity competence;
- succession discipline;
- ecosystem-level convergence;
- explicit stewardship authority.

## 27. Falsifiability

The participation model is weakened or falsified if:

- participation cannot be separated from familiarity;
- capability cannot be separated from authority;
- role boundaries cannot be operationally enforced;
- participation assignments cannot be reproduced;
- domain-bounded authority produces incoherent outcomes;
- computational and human participants require incompatible constitutional principles;
- suspension and revocation cannot preserve historical validity;
- participation records cannot reconstruct material actions.

## 28. Participation Invariants

1. Participation requires explicit authority.
2. Authority is domain-bounded.
3. Authority is role-bounded.
4. Authority is version-relative.
5. Competence does not create authority.
6. Contribution does not create canonical status.
7. Implementation does not create constitutional authority.
8. Evaluation does not create correction authority.
9. Governance does not automatically create stewardship.
10. Participation may be suspended or revoked.
11. Historical participation remains traceable.
12. No role may exceed its explicit scope.
13. Uncertainty shall remain declared.
14. Blocking findings prevent affected participation.
15. Stewardship requires independent qualification.

## 29. Research Result

Canonical participation is established as a verified, evidence-bearing, role-bounded, domain-bounded, version-relative, and revocable constitutional relationship.

Morning Star can therefore distinguish access from competence, competence from eligibility, eligibility from authority, participation from governance, and governance from stewardship.
