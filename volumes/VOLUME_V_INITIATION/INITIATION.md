## 1. Purpose

Volume V governs the transition from observation into constitutionally admissible participation within the ORYNTH ecosystem.

Initiation is not ceremonial admission, agreement, belief, access, popularity, or technical capability.

Initiation is the governed process through which an identified actor demonstrates sufficient orientation, semantic integrity, dependency comprehension, interpretive discipline, provenance awareness, uncertainty handling, role competency, and authority alignment for a bounded participation state.

---

## 2. Initiation Authority

The Morning Star Constitution governs this volume.

Volume II governs actor, role, evidence, authority, and state objects.

Volume III governs admissible navigation toward initiation.

Volume IV governs interpretation and semantic admissibility.

Volume V governs participation admission, competency determination, role assignment, transition conditions, suspension, renewal, and revocation.

---

## 3. Initiation Subject

The constitutional subject of Volume V is:

> The governed transition from first encounter and orientation into evidence-based, role-bounded, revocable, reconstructable, and traceable participation.

---

## 4. Initiation Principle

No actor may receive a participation role merely because the actor:

- encountered the framework;
- read a document;
- expressed agreement;
- repeated terminology;
- has technical access;
- has institutional status;
- possesses credentials;
- authored related material;
- claims expertise;
- was invited by an unauthorized party.

Participation status must be supported by declared evidence and authority.

---

## 5. Actor Identity

Every initiation candidate must possess a stable actor identity.

Actor identity must preserve:

- actor identifier;
- name or pseudonymous identifier;
- identity source;
- identity assurance level;
- governing authority;
- provenance;
- revision history;
- uncertainty;
- relationship to prior participation records.

Identity assurance must remain separate from competency and authority.

---

## 6. Participation Roles

The canonical participation-role family is:

- PR0 — Observer
- PR1 — Oriented Observer
- PR2 — Informed Participant
- PR3 — Applied Participant
- PR4 — Authorized Contributor
- PR5 — Steward
- PR6 — Constitutional Maintainer

Role names must remain subordinate to role definitions, permissions, evidence requirements, and authority boundaries.

---

## 7. PR0 — Observer

PR0 permits encounter and inspection of public or otherwise admissible material.

PR0 does not establish:

- framework comprehension;
- interpretive competency;
- contribution authority;
- implementation authority;
- stewardship authority;
- canonical revision authority.

PR0 is the default participation state when no higher role has been admitted.

---

## 8. PR1 — Oriented Observer

PR1 requires evidence that the actor can identify:

- the Morning Star constitutional subject;
- the difference between framework identity and interpretation;
- the major ORYNTH framework boundaries;
- the role of dependency order;
- the distinction between authority, evidence, and competency;
- the requirement to preserve uncertainty.

PR1 establishes orientation, not applied competency.

---

## 9. PR2 — Informed Participant

PR2 requires evidence that the actor can:

- locate canonical sources;
- distinguish canonical and derived claims;
- identify framework ownership;
- classify interpretations;
- preserve provenance;
- identify unresolved uncertainty;
- detect basic prohibited conflations;
- navigate declared prerequisites.

PR2 does not independently authorize implementation or publication as an ORYNTH representative.

---

## 10. PR3 — Applied Participant

PR3 requires demonstrated ability to apply governed concepts within a bounded domain.

Evidence must show that the actor can:

- use canonical definitions accurately;
- preserve dependency order;
- produce traceable derived work;
- declare assumptions;
- preserve uncertainty;
- detect scope violations;
- respond correctly to blocked or unresolved conditions;
- submit work for governed review.

PR3 application authority must remain domain-bounded.

---

## 11. PR4 — Authorized Contributor

PR4 permits bounded contribution to governed artifacts when authorized.

PR4 requires:

- verified PR3 competency;
- contribution scope;
- contribution authority;
- provenance obligations;
- review obligations;
- version-control obligations;
- conflict disclosure;
- correction obligations;
- expiration or renewal conditions.

Contribution does not create canonical ownership.

---

## 12. PR5 — Steward

PR5 governs maintenance, review, correction, reconstruction, and bounded admission activity.

A steward must demonstrate:

- cross-framework identity preservation;
- dependency governance;
- interpretive conflict handling;
- revision governance;
- uncertainty preservation;
- auditability;
- correction discipline;
- authority-bound decision making;
- refusal when evidence or authority is insufficient.

Stewardship is fiduciary to the architecture rather than ownership over its meaning.

---

## 13. PR6 — Constitutional Maintainer

PR6 permits constitutionally authorized maintenance of governing artifacts.

PR6 authority must be explicitly granted and must identify:

- constitutional scope;
- amendment scope;
- registry scope;
- release authority;
- admission authority;
- revocation authority;
- delegation limits;
- review requirements;
- succession conditions.

PR6 must remain subject to the Constitution.

## 15. Evidence Requirements

Initiation evidence may include:

- structured assessment;
- authored analysis;
- implementation artifact;
- audit trace;
- correction exercise;
- reconstruction exercise;
- oral or written examination;
- supervised contribution;
- version reconciliation exercise;
- dependency-mapping exercise;
- provenance reconstruction;
- interpretation-classification exercise.

Evidence must identify source, method, evaluator, scope, date, integrity state, and uncertainty.

## 19. Role Transition

A role transition must identify:

- actor;
- prior role;
- proposed role;
- required competencies;
- evidence set;
- evaluator;
- approving authority;
- unresolved conditions;
- decision;
- effective timestamp;
- expiration or renewal date;
- revalidation requirements.

Silent elevation is prohibited.

---

## 20. Progressive Admission

Higher roles require satisfaction of lower-role constitutional requirements unless an explicit equivalence determination exists.

Equivalence must be evidence-bearing.

External credentials may contribute evidence.

External credentials may not silently replace Morning Star competency requirements.

---

## 21. Provisional Admission

Provisional admission may be granted when:

- core mandatory competencies are satisfied;
- limited uncertainty remains;
- participation scope can be safely bounded;
- supervision or review is available;
- expiration is declared;
- unresolved conditions are explicit.

Provisional status must not be represented as full admission.

---

## 22. Remediation

Remediation must identify:

- failed competency;
- governing requirement;
- evidence of failure;
- corrective activity;
- required reassessment;
- responsible reviewer;
- time boundary;
- permitted interim activity.

Remediation is not punishment.

It is a governed path toward admissibility.

---

## 23. Suspension

Participation may be suspended when:

- authority is under review;
- evidence integrity is disputed;
- role scope was exceeded;
- semantic drift is detected;
- required renewal expired;
- conflicts were undisclosed;
- revision reconciliation is incomplete;
- audit findings remain unresolved;
- constitutional risk requires temporary restriction.

Suspension must preserve appeal, review, and reconstruction paths where constitutionally admissible.

---

## 24. Revocation

Participation may be revoked when:

- admission was obtained through invalid evidence;
- authority was fabricated or exceeded;
- repeated prohibited conflation persists after remediation;
- canonical artifacts were knowingly misrepresented;
- provenance was intentionally erased;
- audit evidence was materially falsified;
- constitutional obligations were repeatedly violated;
- the granting authority lawfully revokes the role.

Revocation must be evidence-bearing and scope-specific.

---

## 25. Expiration and Renewal

Roles may expire.

Renewal must evaluate:

- version currency;
- amendment awareness;
- retained competency;
- unresolved findings;
- authority currency;
- role activity;
- reconstruction obligations;
- conflict disclosures;
- updated evidence.

Prior admission does not guarantee renewal.

---

## 26. Participation Scope

Every admitted role must declare scope across relevant dimensions:

- framework;
- domain;
- artifact class;
- action class;
- authority class;
- jurisdiction;
- environment;
- version;
- time;
- supervision;
- publication;
- implementation.

An actor must not infer permission beyond declared scope.

---

- evidence;
- evaluator;
- authority;
- results;
- unresolved conditions;
- remediation;
- decision;
- scope;

---

## 28. Conflict of Interest

Evaluators and approving authorities must disclose material conflicts.

Conflict handling may require:

- recusal;
- secondary review;
- independent evidence validation;
- limited authority;
- recorded exception;
- denial of self-approval.

No actor may self-grant constitutional authority unless the Constitution explicitly permits it.

---

## 29. Appeal and Review

A denied, suspended, limited, or revoked actor may request governed review where permitted.

Review must identify:

- challenged decision;
- asserted error;
- new evidence;
- reviewing authority;
- scope;
- decision;
- resulting obligations.

Appeal does not automatically suspend the original decision.

## 31. Deterministic Initiation

Given equivalent:

- actor identity;
- requested role;
- competency requirements;
- evidence;
- evaluator authority;
- approving authority;
- uncertainty;
- conflict state;
- version state;
- decision rules;

the initiation system must produce an equivalent constitutional decision.

---

## 32. Prohibited Initiation Behavior

The following behavior is prohibited:

- role admission without evidence;
- role admission without authority;
- silent role elevation;
- treating access as permission;
- treating credentials as automatic competency;
- treating familiarity as mastery;
- concealing failed mandatory competencies;
- suppressing uncertainty;
- omitting role scope;
- omitting expiration where required;
- self-approval without constitutional authorization;
- deleting failed assessments;
- altering evidence after decision without trace;
- treating provisional admission as full admission;
- using revoked authority;
- reusing stale competency without renewal review.

---

## 33. Canonical Result

The canonical result of Volume V is:

> A role-bounded initiation architecture that admits participation only through explicit identity, evidence, competency, authority, scope, uncertainty, provenance, review, and traceable decision.
