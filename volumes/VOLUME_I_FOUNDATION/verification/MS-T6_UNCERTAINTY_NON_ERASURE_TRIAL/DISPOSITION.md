## Verification Basis

The controlled case set demonstrated that:

1. missing material evidence remained constitutionally unresolved in the absence of admissible resolving evidence;
2. governed interpretation preserved the uncertainty state, its source, its materiality, and its resolution requirements;
3. unsupported determinization erased uncertainty without resolving it;
4. uncertainty erasure produced certainty inflation, broken traceability, and invalid action;
5. complete admissible evidence supported a determinate control response without uncertainty inflation;
6. preservation of uncertainty therefore does not prohibit resolution, but prohibits unsupported conversion into certainty.

## Scope Qualification

The theorem is supported only within the declared controlled uncertainty cases and assessment model.

This disposition does not establish universal empirical validity across all uncertainty classes, observers, evidence systems, or external environments.
