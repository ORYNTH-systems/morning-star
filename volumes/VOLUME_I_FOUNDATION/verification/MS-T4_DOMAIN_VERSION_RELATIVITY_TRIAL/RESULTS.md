## Condition Findings

### Condition A — Same Domain, Same Version

The observer preserved all assessed target properties under the identical governed domain and governing version.

**Disposition:** TRANSFER_VALID

### Condition B — Same Domain, Materially Changed Version

The observer incorrectly applied superseded version knowledge to the materially changed target version. This produced governing-version, authority, uncertainty, transfer-rule, and revalidation errors.

The prior competence therefore generated measurable negative transfer.

**Disposition:** REVALIDATION_REQUIRED

### Condition C — Related Domain

General reconstruction and uncertainty-preservation capabilities transferred, but domain-specific definition, dependency, and authority competence remained incomplete.

The transfer was bounded rather than universal.

**Disposition:** TRANSFER_PARTIAL

### Condition D — Distinct Domain

The observer correctly refused to infer competence or authority in the distinct target domain. Properties requiring target-domain knowledge remained unsupported rather than being falsely reconstructed.

**Disposition:** TRANSFER_INVALID

## Transfer-Distance Effect

Same-domain, same-version preservation exceeded:

- same-domain, materially changed-version preservation by 55.56 percentage points;
- related-domain preservation by 33.33 percentage points;
- distinct-domain property preservation by 44.44 percentage points.

The tested cases therefore exhibited measurable degradation as domain or version distance increased.

## Interpretation

The controlled evidence supports the MS-T4 proposition that observer state is relative to domain and version.

Verified competence in one governed state did not establish universal competence in another state. Transfer remained admissible only where domain identity, version compatibility, capability scope, evidence, and authority conditions were preserved or explicitly bounded.

The materially changed-version condition produced negative transfer when obsolete source-version rules were applied to the target. The related-domain condition supported only partial transfer. The distinct-domain condition required refusal of unsupported competence rather than false equivalence.
