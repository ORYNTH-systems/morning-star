## Verification Basis

The controlled case set demonstrated that:

1. complete required lineage permitted full reconstruction;
2. missing material links prevented full reconstruction;
3. partial lineage remained classified as partial rather than being inflated into completeness;
4. unresolved authority and exclusion properties remained unresolved;
5. missing canonical source, version, dependency, provenance, or result evidence prevented admissible reconstruction;
6. a minimal control remained reconstructable when all links required by its declared schema were present;
7. reconstruction requirements therefore remain relative to the governed object's explicit traceability schema;
8. no interpretation acquired evidentiary lineage that was absent from the controlled record.

## Constitutional Finding

A semantic result cannot be constitutionally treated as reconstructable merely because its conclusion appears understandable or plausible.

Required lineage must remain recoverable through explicit links.

Where a material link is absent:

- the missing property remains unresolved;
- completeness cannot be claimed;
- authority cannot be inferred;
- provenance cannot be invented;
- uncertainty cannot be erased.

## Scope Qualification

The theorem is supported only within the declared controlled canonical object, evidence references, reconstruction cases, traceability chains, responses, and assessment model.

This disposition does not establish universal empirical validity across all semantic objects, observers, frameworks, institutions, or external environments.
