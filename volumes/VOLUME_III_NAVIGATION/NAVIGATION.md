# Morning Star Volume III — Navigation

**Document ID:** MS-V3-NAVIGATION-001  
**Document Type:** Constitutional Volume Specification  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** NAVIGATION FOUNDATION  
**Governing Authority:** Morning Star Constitution  
**Ontology Dependency:** Morning Star Volume II  
**Author:** Ashley S. Harris  

---

## 1. Purpose

Volume III governs how an observer enters, traverses, pauses, resumes, exits, and re-enters the ORYNTH ecosystem without losing canonical identity, dependency order, provenance, uncertainty, or role boundaries.

Navigation is not merely presentation order.

Navigation is a constitutional process that determines whether a participant encounters governed material in an admissible sequence with sufficient context, satisfied prerequisites, explicit unresolved states, and a reconstructable trace.

---

## 2. Navigation Authority

The Morning Star Constitution governs this volume.

Volume II governs the identity and validity of navigated objects.

Volume III may determine admissible pathways between governed objects.

Volume III may not redefine the canonical meaning of those objects.

---

## 3. Navigation Subject

The constitutional subject of Volume III is:

> The governance of dependency-aware movement through canonical objects, frameworks, interpretations, and participation stages from first contact through competent orientation.

---

## 4. Entry Points

An entry point is a governed object through which an observer begins or resumes navigation.

Canonical entry-point classes are:

- EC0 — Unstructured Encounter
- EC1 — Framework-Directed Entry
- EC2 — Question-Directed Entry
- EC3 — Artifact-Directed Entry
- EC4 — Role-Directed Entry
- EC5 — Dependency-Directed Entry
- EC6 — Revision-Directed Re-entry
- EC7 — Reconstruction-Directed Re-entry
- EC8 — Steward-Guided Entry

Every entry point must identify:

- entry-point identifier;
- initiating observer or system;
- encountered object;
- entry class;
- source;
- timestamp;
- assumed context;
- known dependencies;
- unresolved dependencies;
- initial semantic state;
- authority;
- provenance.

---

## 5. First-Contact Governance

At first contact, Morning Star must determine:

1. what object was encountered;
2. whether the object is canonical, derived, provisional, superseded, or unknown;
3. which framework owns the object;
4. what prerequisites govern interpretation;
5. what prior knowledge is asserted;
6. what prior knowledge is evidenced;
7. what uncertainty exists;
8. what navigation paths are admissible;
9. what paths are blocked;
10. what trace must be created.

First contact must not assume comprehension, agreement, competency, or participation authority.

---

## 6. Navigation Path

A navigation path is an ordered set of governed transitions between canonical objects or states.

Every path must identify:

- path identifier;
- origin;
- destination;
- path class;
- required prerequisites;
- conditional prerequisites;
- prohibited transitions;
- authority;
- expected semantic-state changes;
- evidence generated;
- uncertainty behavior;
- completion condition;
- failure condition.

---

## 7. Navigation Classes

The canonical navigation classes are:

- FOUNDATIONAL
- DEPENDENCY_RESOLUTION
- FRAMEWORK_ORIENTATION
- CONCEPTUAL
- EVIDENTIARY
- COMPARATIVE
- ROLE_PREPARATION
- REVISION_RECONCILIATION
- RECONSTRUCTION
- STEWARDSHIP

- REENTRY

Navigation class must remain separate from interpretation class.

---

## 8. Prerequisite Resolution

A prerequisite is satisfied only when its declared satisfaction condition is met.

Prerequisite statuses are:

- NOT_EVALUATED
- SATISFIED
- PARTIALLY_SATISFIED
- UNSATISFIED
- WAIVED_BY_AUTHORITY
- NOT_APPLICABLE
- CONFLICTING
- UNRESOLVED

A recommendation is not a prerequisite.

A prerequisite may not be silently waived.

Every waiver must identify authority, rationale, scope, duration, and downstream consequences.

---

## 9. Dependency-Aware Routing

Navigation must preserve dependency order.

A route may proceed only when:

- required prior objects are identified;
- required semantic states are established;
- required authority is present;
- required evidence is admissible;
- unresolved dependencies are explicitly preserved;
- prohibited transitions are absent.

When multiple valid routes exist, Morning Star may rank them but must not misrepresent preference as constitutional necessity.

---

## 10. Observer-State Assessment

Navigation must assess the observer's current state without converting self-report into unverified competency.

Assessment dimensions may include:

- encountered frameworks;
- identified canonical objects;
- dependency comprehension;
- interpretation classification;
- provenance awareness;
- uncertainty recognition;
- demonstrated competency;
- prior participation state;
- revision currency.

Assessment results must preserve evidence source and uncertainty.

---

## 11. Novice and Expert Navigation

Novice and expert pathways may differ in compression, pacing, and branching.

They may not differ in constitutional requirements.

Expertise may reduce explanatory repetition.

Expertise may not eliminate required identity, dependency, authority, provenance, uncertainty, or revision checks.

---

## 12. Blocked Pathways

A pathway must be blocked when:

- required identity is unresolved;
- a prerequisite is unsatisfied;
- dependency order would be violated;
- authority is absent;
- evidence is insufficient;
- the destination requires a higher role;
- the source object is revoked or invalid;
- an unresolved contradiction materially affects the path;
- revision status is stale;
- navigation would cause prohibited conflation.

A block must produce a traceable reason and a permitted next action where one exists.

---

## 13. Deferred Pathways

A pathway may be deferred rather than blocked when:

- required information is temporarily unavailable;
- a prerequisite is partially satisfied;
- evidence review is pending;
- revision reconciliation is pending;
- authority confirmation is pending;
- unresolved uncertainty does not yet require final refusal.

Deferred status must not be represented as completion.

---

## 14. Return and Re-entry

Re-entry must evaluate whether the prior navigation state remains current.

Re-entry checks include:

- object version changes;
- constitutional amendments;
- dependency changes;
- authority changes;
- role changes;
- interpretation changes;
- unresolved prior conditions;
- drift events;
- superseded artifacts;
- required revalidation.

A previous completion state must not be silently reused when governing conditions have changed.

---

## 15. Navigation Trace

Every governed navigation session must produce a reconstructable trace containing:

- session identifier;
- observer or participant identifier;
- entry point;
- path sequence;
- objects encountered;
- dependencies evaluated;
- prerequisites evaluated;
- decisions;
- blocks;
- deferrals;
- uncertainty;
- authority references;
- state transitions;
- timestamps;
- exit condition;
- resulting semantic state;
- resulting participation state.

---

## 16. Navigation Decision

Canonical navigation decisions are:

- PROCEED
- PROCEED_WITH_UNCERTAINTY
- DEFER
- BLOCK
- REDIRECT
- RECONSTRUCT
- REVALIDATE
- EXIT
- COMPLETE

A navigation decision must include its governing rule and evidence.

---

## 17. Recommendation Boundaries

Morning Star may recommend a path based on:

- constitutional dependency;
- observer state;
- declared objective;
- role requirement;
- revision status;
- evidence availability;
- efficiency among equally admissible routes.

Morning Star must distinguish:

- REQUIRED
- STRONGLY_RECOMMENDED
- OPTIONAL
- ALTERNATIVE
- PROHIBITED

A recommendation may not be represented as authority unless authority exists.

---

## 18. Framework Discovery Order

Framework discovery order must be determined by dependency and subject relationship rather than publication date, popularity, or interface visibility alone.

A framework may require prior orientation to:

- IPT;
- UAA;
- EIP;
- AOMS;
- CIGCS;
- ORYNTH Audit Framework;
- domain-specific frameworks;
- constitutional runtimes;
- future admitted frameworks.

Discovery order must be declared per pathway.

---

## 19. Cross-Framework Navigation

Cross-framework navigation must preserve:

- source framework identity;
- destination framework identity;
- ownership boundaries;
- dependency type;
- shared terminology distinctions;
- authority distinctions;
- interpretation boundaries;
- unresolved conflicts.

Cross-framework navigation must not imply framework equivalence.

---

## 20. Revision-Aware Navigation

When a governed object changes, navigation must determine:

- whether prior pathways remain valid;
- whether prior competency remains current;
- whether interpretations require reconciliation;
- whether dependent objects require revalidation;
- whether role authority remains valid;
- whether historical traces require migration references.

---

## 21. Navigation Failure

Navigation failure occurs when the system cannot produce an admissible path while preserving constitutional invariants.

Failure classes include:

- IDENTITY_FAILURE
- DEPENDENCY_FAILURE
- PREREQUISITE_FAILURE
- AUTHORITY_FAILURE
- PROVENANCE_FAILURE
- UNCERTAINTY_FAILURE
- ROLE_FAILURE
- REVISION_FAILURE
- TRACE_FAILURE
- CONFLATION_FAILURE
- RECONSTRUCTION_FAILURE

Failure must remain distinguishable from refusal, deferral, and unresolved status.

---

## 22. Exit Governance

A navigation session may exit as:

- COMPLETED
- PARTIALLY_COMPLETED
- DEFERRED
- BLOCKED
- WITHDRAWN
- SUPERSEDED
- RECONSTRUCTION_REQUIRED
- REVALIDATION_REQUIRED
- INADMISSIBLE

Exit must preserve unfinished requirements and permissible re-entry conditions.

---

## 23. Deterministic Navigation

Given equivalent:

- canonical object states;
- observer state;
- dependencies;
- authority;
- evidence;
- uncertainty;
- revision state;
- navigation policy;

the navigation engine must produce an equivalent constitutional decision.

Presentation order may vary only where the constitutional decision remains unchanged.

---

## 24. Prohibited Navigation Behavior

The following behavior is prohibited:

- silently skipping prerequisites;
- treating recommendations as requirements;
- treating requirements as optional;
- inferring competency from access;
- inferring authority from competency;
- hiding blocked paths;
- erasing unresolved dependencies;
- routing through superseded objects without warning;
- collapsing frameworks into one generalized concept;
- declaring completion without a trace;
- reusing stale navigation state without revalidation;
- optimizing speed at the expense of constitutional integrity.

---

## 25. Canonical Result

The canonical result of Volume III is:

> A dependency-aware, role-sensitive, revision-aware, uncertainty-preserving navigation architecture capable of moving an observer from first encounter toward competent orientation while preserving canonical identity and producing a complete constitutional trace.
